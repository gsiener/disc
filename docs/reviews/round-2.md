# Blind visual review — round 2

Frames captured at `--q high`, 1920x1080, on a real GPU (ANGLE Metal, Apple M1 Max).
Three critics reviewed them independently. None was told what the project was, who
built it, or on what budget — each was asked first to *guess the product tier from the
pixels alone*, then to score against an anchored rubric where 10 means indistinguishable
from a shipped Madden 26 / NBA 2K26 marketing frame.

Players were still placeholder capsules in these frames, and critics were told to
disregard that.

## The blind guesses

All three independently identified the same failure, unprompted.

**Critic 1 (lighting-atmosphere)** — verdict `PROTOTYPE`

> Engine tech demo — specifically a browser/WebGL (three.js) graphics showcase built by one person or a very small team, not a shipped game.
> 
> The giveaway is the *inversion* between code sophistication and picture quality. The renderer underneath is clearly ambitious: cascaded shadows, GTAO, a real scattering sky, an AgX grade with a proper single-tone-map chain, a turf shader with an analytic band cascade. But the pictures those systems produce contain a car park full of two-box cars, trees made of three icospheres on a cylinder, a city of extruded boxes wearing one tiled window bitmap, a 19:24 sky that is a flat mid-tone card with no sun and no clouds, and a night frame where nothing casts a shadow at all. That combination — deep tech, placeholder world, brief text that promises effects the frame does not show ("long multi-shadows") — is the signature of a rendering demo iterating on systems, not a product being art-directed toward a ship date.
> 
> Nothing here would survive a marketing screenshot review at a AAA sports studio. The night frame could pass at thumbnail size for a low-budget commercial title; the establishing wide could not pass for a student capstone.

**Critic 2 (materials-detail)** — verdict `PROTOTYPE`

> An engine / graphics tech demo — specifically a procedural WebGL (three.js) rendering showcase with no authored art and no art-direction pass. Nothing in these frames was made by an artist: every surface is a runtime-baked procedural, with no photoscans, no hand-painted albedo, no sculpted detail and no placed props. The tell is the *shape* of the competence — AgX tone mapping, cascaded shadow maps, GTAO, aerial perspective, a band-limited mip-aware texture cascade and a genuinely thoughtful thin-lens DOF are all at a level a student project never reaches, while everything that costs art-hours (cloth folds, seat geometry, divots, faces, cast shadows in the stands, chalk in frame) is simply absent. Excellent renderer, zero art budget. Well past a student project, well short of a competent indie's shipped look, and nowhere near a current-gen console sports title.

**Critic 3 (composition-polish)** — verdict `PROTOTYPE`

> Engine tech demo — specifically, a graphics-programmer's stadium/turf renderer, not a game. The tell is the inversion of effort: the bowl geometry, the LED perimeter ring with a real dot-mask shader, the instanced crowd LODs and the band-limited procedural turf are all clearly the work of someone who knows what they are doing, and yet the frame has no depth of field, no aerial perspective, and roughly 40% of the broadcast image is two untextured grey and tan gradients. Shipped games get that ratio the other way around: they fake the hard stuff and never let a flat plane occupy a third of a marketing frame. A student project would not have a band-limited tuft bake or coverage-preserving chalk. A shipped title would not ship an upside-down SUBSTITUTION decal and pepper-noise grass. This is a tech demo with excellent bones and no art direction pass.

## Worst offences, as ranked

### Critic 1 — lighting-atmosphere

1. night.png casts no shadows whatsoever. The shot's own brief promises 'long multi-shadows' from four towers; every capsule in the frame is a flat white pill with no dark side, no cast shadow, and no contact darkening at its base. They are stickers pasted on a green plane. The frame is drowning in unshadowed fill — four RectAreaLights (which physically cannot cast shadows in three.js) at intensity 25 spanning the entire perimeter, plus the tower irradiance being re-injected into the indirect budget in three separate places — so whatever the 0.86-intensity spot shadows contribute is buried under key-strength ambient.
2. The far run-off strip in night.png clips to solid paper white for roughly 900 px, swallowing half the sponsor boards, the bench canopy and every prop in front of them. There is no highlight rolloff at all — it is a hard-edged white smear with a value step at its boundary. A shot whose whole point is 'four-tower specular' has instead produced one blown region and zero speculars.
3. A ~70 m wavelength brightness blotch rolls across the pitch in both broadcast.png and night.png — a soft dark swathe over the near half in broadcast, hard-edged polygonal patches mid-field at night. It reads as a stale lightmap bake or a cloud shadow with no cloud in the sky. Groomed stadium turf does not do this, and it is the single loudest 'this is not real' cue in the two field shots.
4. Nothing outside the bowl casts a shadow in stadium.png. 360 cars, 320 trees, 46 light poles and 27 buildings all sit on the tarmac with zero contact, and the shadow far plane is 160 m while the camera is 212 m from its target — so the establishing shot, the one frame whose entire job is to sell scale, has essentially no cast light in it. The dark streaks readable as shadows in the car park are painted into the ground texture.
5. The sky at 19:24 is a flat, hueless olive-tan card. No sun, no sunset gradient, no cloud structure despite the model being told 36 % coverage, no horizon glow band. The hills behind the venue have been hazed to exactly the sky value, so the horizon is a dead straight ruler line. Meanwhile the shadows say low raking sun and the bowl interior says floodlit night: three different times of day in one frame.
6. Zero aerial perspective inside the bowl. In broadcast.png the far stands at 120 m carry the same contrast, the same saturation and the same black point as the near sideline at 20 m. The one free depth cue a stadium hands you has been thrown away, and the frame reads as a flat cutout stack.
7. The two largest surfaces in broadcast.png — the rubber-crumb apron ring and the straw-tinted outfield beyond it — are featureless. They get a single tiled map with no scale-invariant detail path, so past ~15 m they collapse to their linear mean and become untextured card, while the pitch two metres away keeps full structure. The outfield's silhouette is also visibly scalloped by 8.6 m mesh facets. In night.png that same dead surface is *brighter* than the floodlit pitch — a straight value inversion.
8. Establishing-shot asset quality is placeholder throughout. Cars are a box with a smaller box on top, no wheels. Trees are three subdivision-zero icosahedra on a cylinder, repeated identically 320 times. Buildings are three box prototypes wearing one 512 px window bitmap. The roof is a flat grey slab with a painted grid and no trusses, no material breakup, no specular.

### Critic 2 — materials-detail

1. The pitch surface is legibly WOVEN FABRIC. Magnified, the ground between the blades in turf.png (and the whole 8-15 m band in closeup.png) resolves into a regular basket-weave lattice of pale dashes at roughly 23 mm x 79 mm. It reads as hessian sacking, and it is present in every frame where the ground is inside ~15 m. TurfTextures.ts carries a 40-line comment claiming this exact artifact was eliminated; it was not — the fix addressed only one of the two axes.
2. A million grass blades cast no shadow and contribute no occlusion. Grass.ts sets castShadow=false and opts the entire sward out of GTAO via userData.noAO. There is no root darkening that responds to the sun, no inter-blade self-shadowing, and no contact shadow on the pitch beneath. This is precisely why the turf macro reads as green plastic hair glued onto a printed mat rather than a sward.
3. A ~10x coverage cliff at exactly 4.5 m from camera. Ring 0 carries 5,190 blades/m2, ring 1 carries 119/m2 — a 43x areal density drop against only a 4.5x blade-width compensation. This produces the hard horizontal 'grass line' band visible across turf.png and the flat pea-soup wash the pitch becomes from ~8 m out in closeup.png.
4. The crowd casts no shadows at all and sits on untextured flat planes. crowd/Shader.ts runs a standalone lighting model that never samples any shadow map, and no crowd mesh sets castShadow. At this low sun angle roughly 60% of a real stand would be in cast shadow. SEAT_FRAG has no texture map, no AO term, and a hardcoded sunVis of 0.85 for every seat in the bowl; buildChair() is literally two PlaneGeometry quads with no thickness.
5. The whole frame is rendered at 1 sample per pixel and resolved with morphological AA only — captured at deviceScaleFactor 1, WebGLRenderer antialias:false, and a deliberate TAA bypass in PostFX. A million sub-pixel-wide blades plus hard-stepped crowd stripes at 1 SPP through SMAA is the source of the crawl, fizz and moiré across all three frames.
6. Crowd jersey stripes are raw unfiltered step() functions with no derivative-based antialiasing and no distance retirement, guaranteeing moiré on every banded shirt past ~15 m. Visible in the magnified crop as stair-stepping wherever a stripe crosses a lathe facet edge.
7. Film grain is tuned to peak on exactly the luminance of sunlit grass. The luminance bell maxes at mid-grey, which under AgX is where a sunlit pitch sits — so the largest surface in every frame receives the maximum grain in the frame.
8. The 'closeup' hero shot frames no subject. Camera is 3.66 m from target at 42 deg fov, giving 2.81 m of vertical frame where chest-up needs ~0.75 m — 4x too wide — and the focus plane is set to 3.1 m against a 3.66 m subject distance.
9. Cleat divots do not exist anywhere in the codebase; the wear map is a smooth low-frequency fbm field with no discrete stamps or scuff geometry. The turf shot's brief promises divots, chalk-line edge and mow stripes and delivers none of the three — the camera never gets within 17 m of a chalk line, so the best-authored code in the whole turf shader is not visible in a single frame.
10. Turf micro-detail dies completely between 8 m and 25 m — the magnified mid-field crop of closeup.png is a near-flat green wash with no tuft, no stripe, no clump, no soil break-up, decorated only with isolated one-pixel white specks (far-LOD blades clamped to the width floor) that read as dandruff on the pitch.

### Critic 3 — composition-polish

1. Zero readable depth of field in any of the three frames, despite every shot declaring focus and aperture. The sideline frame is a 20-degree telephoto at f/2.4 focused at 27 m with the far stand at 60 m — that stand should be mush and it is razor sharp. Flat depth is the single loudest 'not a real camera' signal a frame can send, and it is present in all three.
2. No aerial perspective. The far side of the bowl in broadcast.png has the same contrast, the same saturation and the same black point as the near touchline 30 m away. Every AAA stadium frame separates its planes with haze; here 120 m of air does nothing at all, so the whole image collapses onto one depth plane.
3. Roughly 40% of the broadcast frame — the entire lower-left — is an untextured flat grey plane meeting an untextured flat tan plane along a hard, wavy, aliased seam with no kerb, no dirt transition and no grass overhang. It reads as unfinished blockout geometry sitting next to a finished pitch.
4. Black salt-and-pepper speckle scattered across the entire pitch surface in the broadcast frame. It reads as compression artefacting or dead sensor pixels. It is sub-pixel grass blades being width-floored into visible dots at about 4 blades per square metre.
5. Broadcast framing is dead. The horizon is out of frame, the seven players are crammed into a thin horizontal band across the upper-middle third, and the bottom 45% is empty run-off. There is no leading line, no foreground occluder, no depth stack — nothing a broadcast director or a marketing art lead would ever sign off.
6. The mow stripes, which are the cheapest and most reliable 'this is a real pitch' cue in the entire genre, are unreadable. The pitch reads as one mottled olive field with two broad soft bands that look like uneven lighting rather than mowing.
7. The SUBSTITUTION decal on the run-off renders 180 degrees rotated — it literally reads NOITUTITSBUS from the broadcast camera. It is also an 85%-opacity white line-art overlay with zero surface integration, sitting on the untextured apron, which makes it look like a UI element pasted onto the ground.
8. The endline photographers' lens barrels render as polished chrome pipes with hard specular streaks — the most recognisable 'default material' silhouette there is, right in the foreground of the endzone hero shot.
9. The daylight grade is milky. There is no true black and no true white anywhere in broadcast.png or sideline.png; the histogram is compressed into the middle, greens sit at khaki rather than pitch green, and the whole thing reads as an ungraded viewport capture rather than a broadcast look.
10. The endzone shot promises 'celebration, crowd behind, low sun flare' and delivers a crushed-to-black crowd with no rim light, no flare, no bloom and no visible sun. The brief in the shot list is not met by the pixels.

## Actionable fixes, by file

### `src/world/field/TurfMaterial.ts`

**Problem.** The macro cascade's two coarsest octaves (lines 198-199) are `0.66 * fFbm(P * 0.0620)` and `0.46 * fFbm(P * 0.0145)` — wavelengths of roughly 16 m and 69 m — and after `mott *= 0.62` they still swing `col` by about ±17 % via line 264 and push `health` by ±0.4 via line 262. That is the giant soft dark blotch across the pitch in broadcast.png. Separately, line 227's `float k = smoothstep(-0.06, 0.06, mott * 3.0)` makes the two-layer tile selection a near-hard switch whose boundary is an iso-contour of that same 69 m field — which is why night.png shows straight-edged polygonal patches mid-pitch.

**Fix.** Cut the 0.0620 octave amplitude to ~0.22 and the 0.0145 octave to ~0.12, and gate both through the wear map so low-frequency colour variation only appears where play has actually damaged the sward. A groomed pitch's macro variation is mow-stripe-aligned and drainage-line-aligned, not isotropic fbm. For the seam, widen line 227's transition to `smoothstep(-0.35, 0.35, mott * 3.0)` and add a small blue-noise dither to the selection so the boundary breaks up instead of drawing a contour line.

**Problem.** The mow stripes do not read, for two independent reasons. First, `float sCoord = P.x / uStripeWidth` (line 286) bands the lay in X, so the mower passes run goal-to-goal along Z. Every camera in src/capture/Shots.ts sits on the +/-X sideline, so those bands converge toward a vanishing point instead of stacking up the frame — the least legible arrangement possible from these angles. The comment at lines 283-284 asserts the opposite and is simply backwards. Second, the stripe term contributes `col *= 1.0 + aniso * 0.50` with aniso peaking around 0.76, i.e. a +/-38% swing at 2.6 m pitch, while the macro cascade's two coarsest octaves (lines 198-199: 0.66 amplitude at 16 m features and 0.46 at 69 m) drive `col *= 0.90 + 0.30 * (mott*0.5+0.5)` plus the health ramp at lines 262-263. The low-frequency drainage blotching out-contrasts the stripes at broadcast range, so the pitch reads as one mottled field.

**Fix.** Band in Z: `float sCoord = P.y / uStripeWidth + wobble` (P is P.xz, so P.y is world Z) and flip the view term at line 290 from `-Vw.x` to `-Vw.z`. Better still, go to a checker — multiply a Z-banded lay by a coarse X-banded one at ~3x the pitch — which is what most broadcast pitches actually cut and which survives any camera azimuth. Then pull the two coarsest mott amplitudes from 0.66/0.46 down to ~0.34/0.20 so the stripes win the contrast fight, and raise the stripe amplitude at line 294 from 0.50 to ~0.62.

### `src/world/field/Surrounds.ts`

**Problem.** The apron ring (lines 90-98) and the outfield ring (lines 137-144) are plain `MeshStandardMaterial` with one tiled map set and nothing else — no analytic macro band, no wear, no world-space variation. At the broadcast camera's grazing angle both mip straight to their linear mean, so the largest surface in the frame is a flat colour field. `ringGrid(380, ..., 88)` (line 126) also gives 8.6 m cells carrying a 0.62 m sine displacement, which is why the outfield silhouette is visibly scalloped. And `color: linearColor(0xd2c9a4)` makes the outfield lighter than the pitch, which is why it is brighter than floodlit turf at night.

**Fix.** Give both rings the same treatment the pitch already has: extract the analytic macro cascade from `TurfMaterial.ts` into a shared chunk and chain it onto the apron and outfield materials, so they hold structure at 40 m instead of dying at 15. Add tyre tracks, standing-water staining and a painted perimeter line to the apron — it is 25 % of the broadcast frame and currently carries zero information. Raise `ringGrid` cell density near the pitch (or taper the sine displacement to zero inside 120 m) to kill the scalloped edge. Drop the outfield tint toward `0xa8a888` so it never out-values the pitch.

**Problem.** The apron ring (apronMat, lines 90-98) and the outfield (outMat, lines 137-144) are plain MeshStandardMaterials with a 2.2 m and 2.6 m baked tile respectively. TurfMaterial.ts exists precisely because baked tiles mip to a flat mean past a few metres — it carries five octaves of analytic world-space fbm from 0.3 m up to 70 m for exactly this reason (see the 'analytic macro cascade' block, lines 186-200). Surrounds.ts got none of it. At the 40-90 m these surfaces sit at in broadcast.png, both mip straight to meanAlbedo and render as untextured gradients occupying roughly 40% of the frame. The outfield's `color: linearColor(0xd2c9a4)` beige tint on top makes the grass outfield read as sand.

**Fix.** Give apronMat and outMat the same `chainCompile` shader injection makeTurfMaterial uses. At minimum port the two coarsest analytic octaves — `mott += 0.66 * fFbm(P * 0.0620 + 21.0, 3)` and `mott += 0.46 * fFbm(P * 0.0145 + 47.0, 2)` — and multiply diffuseColor by `0.86 + 0.28 * (mott*0.5+0.5)`. For the apron add a second band around P*0.35 for tyre tracks and puddle staining, and a darkening gradient toward the kerb where foot traffic packs the crumb. For the outfield drop the 0xd2c9a4 tint toward 0xb8bfa0 so it reads as unmown grass rather than sand, and add cross-mow banding at a coarser pitch than the field's.

### `src/world/stadium/Exterior.ts`

**Problem.** Every exterior object opts out of shadow casting: buildings line 79, trees line 107, poles line 126, cars line 155 — all `castShadow = false`. Combined with `Lighting.ts` clamping `csm.maxFar` to `CSM_MAX_FAR = 160` (line 41/219) while the stadium shot's camera sits 212 m from its target, the entire establishing frame is effectively unlit by direct sun. The dark bands readable as shadows in the car park are the approach roads painted into `bakeGroundPlan`, not cast light.

**Fix.** Turn `castShadow = true` on cars and trees at least (they are the objects the eye checks for grounding) and raise `CSM_MAX_FAR` to ~700 with a fifth cascade specifically for establishing shots — or, cheaper: add a dedicated far ortho shadow map bound only when the active shot's subject distance exceeds 160 m. If the budget genuinely will not take it, at minimum bake a per-instance contact shadow quad under each car and tree: 360 unlit quads is nothing and it is the difference between parked and floating.

**Problem.** `buildCar` (lines 198-207) is literally two `BoxGeometry` instances — no wheels, no glass separation, no roof taper — instanced 360 times. `buildTree` (lines 170-182) is a cylinder plus three `IcosahedronGeometry(1, 0)` blobs, instanced 320 times with only scale and yaw variation, so the same three-lobe silhouette repeats across the whole horizon. `buildHills` (lines 228-234) bakes a hardcoded cool blue-grey haze (0.52, 0.60, 0.74) into vertex colours regardless of time of day, so at a warm 19:24 the hills neither match the sky nor separate from it — they vanish and leave a ruler-straight horizon.

**Fix.** Cars: add a wheel-arch cut, four dark wheel quads and a windscreen plane with a distinct roughness — a five-minute change that is worth more to this frame than any post effect. Trees: build 3-4 distinct silhouettes (columnar, spreading, young) and mix them; the repetition is more damaging than the poly count. Hills: drive the haze colour from `AERIAL.uAerialHoriz` at build/update time instead of the hardcoded constant, and pull the haze weight down from 0.75 to ~0.5 so a ridgeline still reads. A visible ridge is what tells the eye the venue sits in a place.

### `src/render/Sky.ts`

**Problem.** `EXPOSURE_TRACK = 0.85` (line 48) multiplies every sky quantity — dome radiance, sun disc, horizon, zenith, aerial-perspective colours — by `exposure^-0.85`, then the grade multiplies the frame back up by `exposure`. The comment frames this as making times of day distinguishable; the result in stadium.png is the opposite. Because the round trip passes through the grade's log-space contrast (1.14 about 18 % grey) and the AgX shoulder, it is not a neutral operation: it lands every sky in the same narrow, desaturated slice of the curve. The 19:24 sky has no sun, no gradient and no cloud structure despite the tuning table specifying 36 % coverage at that elevation.

**Fix.** Drop `EXPOSURE_TRACK` to ~0.35 and instead protect the sky at the *grade* stage — a highlight-desaturation shoulder in `Grade.ts` that rolls the sky's hue off rather than a pre-exposure scale that flattens its internal contrast. Then verify by histogram: an establishing frame at 19:24 should show 3-4 stops between the zenith and the horizon band. Right now it shows well under one. Separately, check why the cloud raymarch contributes nothing at coverage 0.36 — an establishing shot with a bald sky has no chance of reading as a real venue.

**Problem.** AERIAL_SCALE = 0.32 (line 29) throttles aerial perspective to nothing. At the broadcast hour the tuning table gives fogDensity ~0.0014, so the scene density is 0.00045/m. At the far stand — 120 m out, camera 15.5 m up, heightFalloff ~0.0135 — the optical depth is 0.00045 * 120 * ~0.78 = 0.042, and after maxOpacity 0.88 the far stand is mixed toward the sky by about 3.7%. That is below the noise floor of the grade. The whole depth-cue budget of the frame is being spent and returning nothing.

**Fix.** Raise AERIAL_SCALE to ~1.3. That puts the far stand at ~14% and the far corner of the roof at ~18%, which is what a real 120 m sightline at golden hour looks like, while the near touchline (30 m) still only picks up ~4% and stays clean and saturated. Verify against endzone.png specifically — the crowd there is 40-70 m out and needs the haze to lift out of the crushed blacks. If a single global scale fights the ground-level turf macro shot, expose it as a per-shot multiplier on the Shot interface in src/capture/Shots.ts rather than tuning it down globally.

### `src/capture/Shots.ts`

**Problem.** Two shots do not deliver the brief written in their own `about` field. (1) `closeup` (line 39): pos [2.1, 1.72, 3.0] against target [0, 1.62, 0] is 3.66 m of subject distance; at fov 42 that frames 2.81 m vertically, roughly 4x too wide for the stated 'chest-up on a receiver' — and `focus: 3.1` puts the focal plane 0.56 m in front of the subject, so even a finished character would be soft. The delivered frame is a wide of empty field with a small capsule in the left third. (2) `turf` (line 59): pos [0.9, 0.16, 1.5] to target [-1.2, 0.03, -1.6] sits ~17 m from the nearest chalk line (sidelines are at |x| = 18.5, per world/grass/shader.ts:70), so the 'chalk line edge' in the brief is not in frame — which means the analytic SDF chalk with its coverage-preserving box filter and overspray bead in TurfMaterial.ts:309-344, the single best-authored piece of code in the turf stack, appears in no frame at all. 'Cleat divots' are also promised and do not exist anywhere in the codebase: WearMap.ts seeds only smooth low-frequency fbm with no discrete stamps.

**Fix.** (1) Change `closeup` to `pos: [0.60, 1.70, 0.86], target: [0, 1.66, 0], fov: 38, focus: 1.05, aperture: 3.4` — 1.05 m at 38 deg frames 0.72 m vertically, which is chest-up, with the focal plane on the subject. (2) Change `turf` to straddle the sideline, e.g. `pos: [19.6, 0.16, 1.5], target: [17.4, 0.03, -1.6]`, so the chalk band, the antialiased overspray and the wear cut all cross the near field where the macro camera can actually resolve them. (3) Add discrete divots to WearMap.ts: a `stampDivot(x, z, yaw)` that writes a 12 x 4 cm anisotropic blob into R (wear) and B (mud) with a hard leading edge, called from the same footstep event GrassSystem already listens for at Grass.ts:231, plus ~200 seeded at init in the middle lane and the goal-line bands. Without discrete stamps the wear map can only ever airbrush, which is exactly what the brown smears in closeup.png look like.

**Problem.** The broadcast shot — pos [-34, 15.5, 30], target [0, 1.6, 4], fov 34 — is badly composed. Aiming a 15.5 m high camera at a target 1.6 m off the deck tips the frustum down far enough to push the horizon and the top of the far stand out of frame, and dedicates the bottom 45% of the image to empty run-off and outfield. The seven players end up compressed into a thin horizontal band across the upper-middle third with no foreground element, no leading line and no depth stack. The endzone shot has the matching problem in reverse: pos [4, 2.2, -46] target [-1, 1.7, -54] points at the endline and away from any sun, so the 'low sun flare' the `about` field promises cannot possibly appear.

**Fix.** For broadcast, raise the target to roughly [-4, 5.0, -2] and pull the camera back and down to about [-42, 12.5, 24] with fov 30. That puts the far touchline and the first tier of stands across the top third, the players in the middle third, and the near touchline plus a sideline prop (bench, cooler, camera operator) at the bottom of frame as a foreground occluder — which also finally gives the DOF near-lobe something to do. For endzone, rotate the shot so the sun sits in or just outside the upper frame edge: at hour 18.9 check Solar.ts for the azimuth and place pos/target so the sun rakes across the LED board, then raise BLOOM_STRENGTH conditionally or lower BLOOM_KNEE_NIGHT in src/render/PostFX.ts so the rig and the wet board edge actually bloom.

### `src/world/stadium/Sideline.ts`

**Problem.** The SUBSTITUTION decal renders 180 degrees rotated — it reads NOITUTITSBUS from the broadcast camera and is equally wrong from the +X bench side, so it is wrong from every viewpoint. The cause is the interaction of `c.rotate(-Math.PI / 2)` in subBoxMaterial (lines 339 and 348), CanvasTexture's default flipY = true (canvasTexture in src/util/Tex.ts never sets it), and `decal.rotation.x = -Math.PI / 2` at line 175. Separately, the decal is a 4.4 x 26 m plane at 85% opacity with depthWrite:false and polygonOffset — pure white line art with no wear, no grain erosion, no paint bead — sitting on the untextured apron, which is why it reads as a UI overlay rather than as paint.

**Fix.** Change both `c.rotate(-Math.PI / 2)` calls to `c.rotate(Math.PI / 2)`, or equivalently add `decal.rotation.z = Math.PI` after line 175, and re-shoot broadcast to confirm. Then give the decal the treatment TurfMaterial.ts already gives the pitch lines: erode coverage with an fbm grain term the way `cov *= mix(0.66, 1.0, smoothstep(-0.42, 0.42, grain))` does at line 320, drop opacity to ~0.6, and perturb the normal along the bead edge so the paint sits on the surface. Ideally kill the plane entirely and fold the sub-box segments into `chalkNearest` alongside the regulation lines, which already has all of this machinery.

**Problem.** The endline photographers' lens barrels (lines 166-167: two CylinderGeometry instances, 0.24 m and 0.30 m diameter, scaled to 0.8 m and 0.16 m long, pushed into the `hard` buffer) render as polished chrome pipes in the foreground of endzone.png. They take M.paint from src/world/stadium/Materials.ts (line 112): roughness 0.45, metalness 0.05, map: sA, normalMap: sN — and that steel map set is UV'd in world metres, so a 25 cm prop samples a ~0.25 m slice of a 256-square texture magnified roughly 40x. The grime and rust noise becomes a smeared metallic streak down the barrel. Roughness 0.45 then catches the perimeter RectAreaLights from Screens.ts (setNight, line 322, intensity 3.0 + k * 22.0) at about 1 m range and blows a hard specular highlight along the whole cylinder.

**Fix.** Two parts. In Sideline.ts, pass a `uvScale` on these Parts — the Part type in Geo.ts already supports it and Screens.ts uses it on the jumbo cases (uvScale: 0.6) — set it to ~8 so the steel grain lands at prop scale instead of building scale. In Materials.ts, add a `matte` material variant (roughness 0.82, metalness 0.0, normalScale 0.2) and route camera bodies, lens barrels and tripods to it; roughness 0.45 is a lacquered-metal value and nothing on a broadcast camera is lacquered. Audit every CYL push in Sideline.ts the same way — the boom mic at line 151 and the cart wheels at line 216 have the same problem.

### `src/world/stadium/Screens.ts`

**Problem.** Four RectAreaLights (line 288, `new THREE.RectAreaLight(0xd8e4ff, 9.0, w, boardH * 1.6)`) are sized `2 * (BOWL.hz - BOWL.cornerR)` metres wide — i.e. each is an 80+ m long emitter — and `setNight` (line 323) drives them to `3.0 + k * 22.0` = 25 at full night. three.js RectAreaLights cannot cast shadows, ever, so this is 25 nits of pure unshadowable fill from a source running the entire touchline. ### `src/render/lighting/Ambient.ts`

**Problem.** The floodlight rig's irradiance is folded into the indirect budget three separate times: `RIG_BOUNCE * towerIrradiance` in the budget itself (line 108/252), `FLOOD_COLOR * towerIrradiance * 0.20` into the ground-bounce colour (lines 239-241), and `towerFill * 0.055` into the probe's lower hemisphere (lines 192-193). With `TowerRig.irradiance` ≈ 1.56 at night, the indirect term ends up comparable to the direct term — which is exactly the ratio at which a shadow stops being visible. **Fix.** Pick one path. Keep the ground-bounce term (it is the physically real one — light off the pitch), delete `RIG_BOUNCE` from the budget and delete `towerFill` from the probe. Then re-tune so the night key:fill ratio lands around 6:1 measured on a test sphere at pitch centre — a floodlit stadium is a *hard* lighting environment, not a softbox. Add a debug render mode that outputs direct-only and indirect-only so the ratio is checkable rather than guessed.

### `src/render/lighting/Towers.ts`

**Problem.** `tierFor()` (lines 53-59) makes only 1-3 of the 4 spots shadow-casting below `ultra`, and `spot.shadow.radius = 2.2` (line 383) is a fixed PCF radius applied to a 1024-2048 map covering a ~37 m disc at a 100 m throw. ### `src/render/sky/Aerial.ts`

### `src/world/field/TurfTextures.ts`

**Problem.** The burlap weave. The `warp` phase-displacement term (line 148) is added only to `s` (the lay-band coordinate, line 155) and NOT to `segF` (the perpendicular tuft-cut coordinate, line 158). So the lay bands wander irregularly while the perpendicular dashes sit on a perfectly regular grid. One irregular axis crossed by one perfectly regular axis is, by definition, warp and weft — that is why the pitch reads as hessian. The `sin(pi*segT)^0.55` taper the comment credits with fixing this softens the dash ENDS but does nothing to the dash PERIODICITY, which is what the eye locks onto. LAY[].sk/sl magnitudes are ~12.6 per tile = 79 mm dashes against 23 mm bands, and at the ground-level turf camera (0.57 mm/px) that is a 138 x 40 px lattice — fully resolved, exactly the scale of the visible weave.

**Fix.** Add a second, decorrelated warp field and apply it to the segment coordinate: add `const fWarpC = lowResField(11, 3, 1013);` beside the others at line 109, then change line 158 to `const segF = L.sk * u + L.sl * v + sampleLR(fWarpC, u, v) * 0.9 + sampleLR(fWarpA, v, u) * 0.5;` (note the swapped u/v on fWarpA so it is decorrelated from the lay warp). Then break the dash-length regularity: at line 168 make the taper exponent per-tuft, `Math.pow(Math.sin(Math.PI * segT), 0.35 + 0.9 * hash2(id, seg, L.seed + 31))`. Finally drop the |sk,sl| magnitudes in the LAY table (line 95-99) from ~12.6 to ~7 so the dash length (140 mm) is no longer a small-integer multiple of the 23 mm band width — a 3.4:1 ratio beats between the two axes and reinforces the lattice; an irrational-ish ratio does not.

### `src/world/Grass.ts`

**Problem.** Line 213 `mesh.castShadow = false` and line 221 `mesh.userData.noAO = true` mean a million blades produce zero occlusion — no self-shadowing, no AO onto the pitch, no contact darkening at the roots. The pitch beneath the sward is lit exactly as brightly as open ground. This is the single largest reason the turf macro reads as plastic hair on a printed mat, and it is also why the sward has no depth: every blade is uniformly lit top to bottom regardless of how many blades stand between it and the sun.

**Fix.** Two changes, neither of which requires shadow-casting a million triangles. (a) Feed the grass coverage back into the pitch: GrassSystem already bakes `turfMap` with coverage in .a (line 130, `bakeTurfMap`). Expose it and add a uniform to TurfMaterial, then in TurfMaterial.ts:307 multiply `gAO *= mix(1.0, 0.40, cover * (1.0 - smoothstep(0.0, 8.0, length(cameraPosition - W))))` so the ground darkens under dense sward within 8 m. (b) Move the root darkening in grass/shader.ts:246 out of the albedo and into the lighting. Right now `col = mix(col * mix(0.44,0.88,farLod), col*1.14, smoothstep(0,0.55,t))` darkens the vertex COLOUR, so it does not respond to sun direction and it inverts with distance. Instead pass `t` through (it is already in vGrassAux.x) and in GRASS_FRAG_LIGHT multiply `reflectedLight.indirectDiffuse` and the wrapped-diffuse term by `mix(0.35, 1.0, smoothstep(0.0, 0.5, vGrassAux.x))`. That is real occlusion, it tracks the key light, and it costs one multiply.

**Problem.** The coverage cliff at 4.5 m. ### `src/world/crowd/Shader.ts`

**Problem.** Three separate material failures in one file. (1) CROWD_FRAG (line 415) is a fully standalone lighting model that never samples a shadow map, and no crowd mesh in Crowd.ts sets castShadow — so at this low sun angle not one of ~8,000 spectators casts a shadow onto the person behind them, onto their seat, or onto the deck. `vSun` (line 401) is computed only from the stand's outward azimuth and the roof line, which cannot produce row-to-row occlusion. (2) SEAT_FRAG (line 539) has NO texture map, NO AO term, and a hardcoded `irradiance(N, vW, 0.85)` — every seat in the bowl gets identical 85% sun regardless of the stand's own shade. (3) Line 447 `base = mix(base, vAlt.rgb, step(0.5, fract(vUv.y * vAlt.w)))` is a raw unfiltered step with 3 or 5 bands per torso and no derivative AA and no distance retirement — guaranteed moiré on every banded shirt.

### `tools/capture.mjs`

**Problem.** Line 104 `defaultViewport: { width: W, height: H, deviceScaleFactor: 1 }` renders the frame at exactly 1920x1080 with one sample per pixel. Combined with core/Engine.ts:29 `antialias: false` and render/PostFX.ts:295 deliberately bypassing TAA (the comment explains the reasoning, and the reasoning is sound for a live frame but not for a marketing capture), the only antialiasing in the pipeline is SMAA — a morphological pass that reconstructs edges from already-aliased luminance and can do nothing about sub-pixel geometry. With a million ~1.3 px blades, hard-stepped crowd stripes and a 12 cm chalk line, the frame is aliasing everywhere before SMAA ever sees it. Note also that Ctx.ts sets `taa: true` at both the high and ultra tiers, but PostFX ignores the flag entirely — it is dead configuration.

**Fix.** For the capture rig specifically, set `deviceScaleFactor: 2` and downsample the grab to 1920x1080. That is a 4x supersampled resolve and it fixes blade crawl, crowd stripe moiré, the chalk edge and the roof-truss aliasing in one change with no shader work. Engine.ts:173-177 already honours devicePixelRatio in capture mode, so this needs no engine change beyond raising `maxDpr` in the ultra preset (Ctx.ts:106) from 1.5 to 2 for the capture path. Separately, either wire a real jittered-projection TAA into PostFX (jitter the projection matrix, accumulate with velocity reprojection from the existing MotionBlurPass velocity, keep the depth attachment intact — the objection in the comment applies to three's TAARenderPass, not to a hand-rolled jitter) or delete the `taa` field from QualitySettings so it stops implying a capability that does not exist.

### `src/render/post/Film.ts`

**Problem.** Lines 75-76: `float bell = 4.0 * y * (1.0 - y); bell *= bell;` peaks at exactly y = 0.5. Under AgX a sunlit pitch lands at roughly 0.45-0.55 luma, so the grain is at maximum strength on the single largest surface in every frame in the shot list. The comment reasons correctly that grain should avoid highlights and crushed blacks, but the consequence in a sports title — where 50-70% of every frame is midtone green — is that the pitch is the noisiest thing on screen. It is a meaningful contributor to the fizzy, unresolved look of the turf and closeup frames.

**Fix.** Bias the bell off mid-grey and exempt saturated green: `float bell = 4.0*y*(1.0-y); bell *= bell; float grassy = smoothstep(0.06, 0.20, col.g - max(col.r, col.b)); bell *= (1.0 - 0.65 * grassy);` — grain stays in the crowd, the concrete and the sky where it reads as sensor noise, and comes off the pitch where it reads as dirt on the lens. Also consider dropping the base uGrain (line 35) from 0.024 to ~0.016; at 1 SPP the render is already carrying its own noise floor and the two compound.

### `src/render/post/Dof.ts`

**Problem.** DOF is numerically inert, and for the broadcast shot the pass is disabled outright. With scale=0.0011 (line 39) and the broadcast shot (fov 34, aperture 0.9, focus 46), s = 1080 * 0.0011 * 0.9 * (1/tan^2(17deg)) = 11.4, so update() at line 143 returns `s / this.focus > 0.8` -> 11.4/46 = 0.25 -> false, and the pass never runs. ### `src/world/grass/shader.ts`

**Problem.** Line 181, `bw = max(bw, min(dist * uPxScale * 1.3, uBlade.y * uWidthScale * 3.2))`, floors every blade at 1.3 px wide. The comment says this prevents crawling noise; at outer-ring densities it guarantees the opposite. planRings in src/world/grass/scatter.ts gives ring 2 the span 22 m to 110 m with SHARE[2] = 0.15 of a 1,000,000 blade budget — 150,000 blades over pi*(110^2 - 19^2) = 36,900 m^2, i.e. 4.1 blades per square metre.
